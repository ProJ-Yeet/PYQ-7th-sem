    opens with the \textbf{question exactly as the paper prints it}, the paper's own
    spelling and slips included, so it can be attempted from this PDF alone. Solutions
    are worked in full: what was calculated, why, and from which formula.\\[8pt]
    ``Solved Problems'' rather than ``Numerical Problems'': in AI most of these are not
    numerical. They are crypt-arithmetic puzzles, resolution proofs, semantic nets to
    draw, search trees to expand and gate networks to train. The ones that \emph{are}
    numerical $-$ Bayes, entropy and information gain, GA fitness, fuzzy inference,
    perceptron and Hebbian weights, Hopfield matrices, forward and back propagation $-$
    sit in the same files.\\[8pt]
    \textbf{Chapter 1 sets no problems} in 41 papers, so it does not appear here.
    Theory lives in the companion volume,
    \textbf{Artificial Intelligence --- Complete Exam Notes}.

exhaustive search (which also reports that \textbf{seven of the thirteen puzzles have

a first-order resolution prover, Bayes posteriors, entropy and information gain, GA
