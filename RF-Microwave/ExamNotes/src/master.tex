\begin{minipage}{0.86\textwidth}\small\color{sub}
\mk{Read the marks column, not the hours column.} Chapter 6 is \textbf{22.4\,\% of every
mark ever set} against a 22\,\% hour share, so it is the one chapter that is exactly as big
as it looks, and it is the biggest. Chapter 1 is \textbf{3 syllabus hours} and
\textbf{9.7\,\%} of the marks, 1.5 times its hour share, because ``compare microwave with
conventional circuits'' alone is asked in \textbf{18 of the 24 papers}. Chapter 4 is 8 hours
for 20.3\,\%, slightly over its share, because the magic-tee family (identify the device,
the radar power problems) is filed with the tee junctions. Nothing here is safe to skip,
because the least-asked chapter, Chapter 3, still appears in \textbf{14 of 24 papers}.\\[6pt]
The marks column sums to 1926 against a theoretical $24\times80 = 1920$, because a two-part
question whose halves belong to different chapters, such as 82 Bh's $[4+8]$ on hazards
\emph{and} on a BTS survey instrument, is counted into both. The percentage each chapter
prints for itself was computed on an archive total of 1902, taken before the Chapter-7
recount, so every one of them runs a shade high. Treat these as shares, not as an audit.
\end{minipage}
